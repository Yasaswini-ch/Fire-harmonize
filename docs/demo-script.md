# Demo video script (about 2 minutes)

Record the screen with the live link. Say the sentences, do the action. Numbers below are from the current export; re-check them
on screen before you record (the app shows them).

**0:00 to 0:15, the problem.** On the opening page. "Satellites have counted fires since 2000, but MODIS and VIIRS count fire
differently, so adding them creates a false jump in 2012. We harmonize them into one record." Hover the globe, click a region card.

**0:15 to 0:35, the result.** The workbench opens on the headline numbers. "For Zambia, 114 of 292 months are estimates. The
latest month is plus 96 percent against normal, and it is provisional." Point at the Now vs normal number in the stat card. If you
want the plain-language version on screen, open the "Results and method" tab under the header: it holds "Results and how to read the graphs" and "How the numbers are made".

**0:35 to 1:05, the calendar.** Scroll to the calendar. "Years across, months down. Diagonal lines are MODIS estimates, the white
line is where VIIRS observations begin, pink outlines are the five highest months." Hover a hatched cell to show its range.

**1:05 to 1:25, your own area.** In "Area and dates" click Draw rectangle and draw a small box on the map. Drag the month slider.
"Any area inside a processed region works. The range gets wider for small areas, and the page says so."

**1:25 to 1:50, trust.** Open Accuracy and methods. "We tested it by leaving each year out: about 11 percent error region-wide
and about 18 to 21 percent for small areas in Zambia. In Western North America small areas are much noisier, and we show that
too." Show the three accuracy graphs and the size table.

**1:50 to 2:00, close.** Open Extras, press Play 30 seconds on Listen. "You can even listen to it: a hollow chord is an estimate,
and the wider the gap, the wider the uncertainty." End on the title and the link.

Do not claim one accuracy figure. Say "solid at region scale, wider uncertainty for small areas."
